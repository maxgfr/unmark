Wildfires are becoming more frequent and severe across many temperate and Mediterranean-type ecosystems, yet the recovery of belowground communities after fire remains poorly characterised, particularly in areas left without post-fire management. Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) are central to plant establishment, nutrient acquisition and soil aggregation, and their persistence after fire may determine whether passive recovery is sufficient or active restoration is required. We assessed mycorrhizal colonisation and soil nutrient status in unmanaged fire-impacted soils from two sites designated as restoration targets, comparing burned plots with adjacent unburned reference plots approximately two years after fire. At each site, soil cores were collected along stratified transects and analysed for pH, total carbon and nitrogen, available phosphorus, exchangeable cations and electrical conductivity. AMF inoculum potential was quantified using a trap-plant bioassay with Sorghum bicolor, and root colonisation was measured by the gridline-intersect method; spore density was determined by wet sieving and sucrose centrifugation. Burned soils showed elevated pH and available phosphorus at both sites, while total nitrogen and organic carbon were reduced at the site that experienced higher burn severity. Mycorrhizal colonisation of trap plants was significantly lower in burned soils than in reference soils (by 38% and 61% at the two sites, respectively), and spore densities followed a similar pattern. Colonisation was negatively associated with available phosphorus and positively associated with organic carbon across all plots. These results indicate that fire-driven shifts in soil chemistry and the loss of fungal propagules persist in the absence of management, and that the degree of impairment differs between sites. We suggest that restoration planning should include site-specific assessment of mycorrhizal inoculum potential and consider targeted inoculation or topsoil transfer where native AMF communities are depleted.
