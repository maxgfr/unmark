Aptasensors are biosensing devices built on aptamers, which are short, single-stranded DNA or RNA sequences with high binding specificity. They have emerged as promising analytical tools for detecting residual antimicrobial agents in food matrices. Detecting antimicrobial residues, including antibiotics, antiparasitics, and anticonvulsants, is critical for food safety and regulatory compliance. Optical aptasensors use optical properties such as fluorescence, surface plasmon resonance, or colorimetric changes. Compared with conventional methods, they offer enhanced selectivity, rapid response times, cost-effectiveness, and minimal sample preprocessing requirements.

Recent work has focused on integrating aptasensors with nanotechnologies (gold nanoparticles, quantum dots, and graphene) to amplify optical signals and improve detection limits. These hybrid platforms show superior sensitivity for low-concentration antimicrobial residues while keeping species-specific recognition. Rapid on-site testing and high-throughput screening have been demonstrated for common agricultural antimicrobials.

Challenges remain in multiplexing detection, standardizing protocols across diverse food matrices, and achieving regulatory validation for commercial deployment. The growing interest in optical aptasensor development reflects the industry's urgent need for practical, accurate, and field-deployable solutions to monitor antimicrobial residues in food supply chains, particularly in response to increasing consumer concerns about food safety and antimicrobial resistance.
