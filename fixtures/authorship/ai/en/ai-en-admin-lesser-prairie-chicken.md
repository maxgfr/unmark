The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has determined that the lesser prairie-chicken (Tympanuchus pallidicinctus), a grassland grouse native to the southern Great Plains, warrants protection under the Endangered Species Act. The Service identified two distinct population segments and assigned each a separate status based on its condition and outlook.

The Northern Distinct Population Segment, which occupies portions of Kansas, Oklahoma, Colorado, and the northeast Texas Panhandle, is listed as threatened. A rule issued under section 4(d) of the Act accompanies this listing and tailors the prohibitions that apply to the segment. Under the 4(d) rule, routine agricultural activities on existing cultivated lands and grazing conducted under a Service-approved management plan are not considered prohibited take.

The Southern Distinct Population Segment, found in eastern New Mexico and the southwest Texas Panhandle, is listed as endangered. This segment is smaller, more isolated, and faces greater risk from habitat loss and drought.

The primary threats to the species are the conversion and fragmentation of native grasslands, encroachment by woody vegetation, energy development, and extended drought. The Service will continue working with landowners, states, and industry on voluntary conservation agreements that benefit the species.
