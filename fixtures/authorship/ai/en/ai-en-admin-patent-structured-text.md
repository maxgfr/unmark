Applicants filing patent applications electronically may submit the specification, claims and abstract in a structured text format, such as DOCX, instead of or in addition to a PDF. Structured text allows the Office to validate content automatically at the time of filing, detect common formatting and content issues early, and process the application more efficiently throughout examination and publication.

When an application is filed in structured text, the text version is treated as the source or evidentiary copy of the application. This means that the document as submitted in structured text is the authoritative record of what was filed, and it is the version relied upon for determining the content of the application as of the filing date. A PDF rendering generated by the Office from the submitted file is provided for the applicant's review and convenience, but it does not take precedence over the structured text submission.

Applicants should review the generated rendering carefully before completing their submission. If a discrepancy is identified between the submitted text and the rendering, the applicant may request that the Office rely on the original structured text document. Such requests should be made promptly and must identify the discrepancy clearly.

Applicants who choose not to file in structured text may still submit documents in PDF format, although a non-electronic filing surcharge or similar fee may apply depending on the filing route.

For technical guidance on acceptable file formats, supported features and validation feedback, please consult the electronic filing system help pages or contact the Electronic Business Center.
