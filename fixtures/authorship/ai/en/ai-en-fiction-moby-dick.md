The morning broke grey and tempestuous upon the waters, and the crew of the Pequod assembled upon the quarterdeck with a solemn countenance. Captain Ahab stood before them, his ivory leg planted firm upon the planking, his dark countenance bearing that terrible fixity of purpose which had become his hallmark. Behind him, the vast ocean stretched in every direction, indifferent to the passion that consumed the hearts of men. The whale—that accursed white leviathan—had been sighted but two hours hence, moving northward with the purposeful certainty of a creature possessed of malign intelligence. Starbuck approached the captain with measured steps, his honest face troubled. "Sir," he ventured, "the men grow weary of this pursuit. The hold is yet well-stocked; we might turn toward home with honour enough for our voyage." But Ahab's reply was terrible in its quiet intensity. He fixed upon his first mate a gaze that seemed to pierce through flesh to the very soul itself. "Home?" he uttered, as though the word were an obscenity. "What home awaits a man consumed by a singular purpose? That accursed cetacean has robbed me of more than limb—it has taken my very essence. I shall not rest, nor shall any man aboard this vessel rest, until that white hump lies beneath our harpoons."
