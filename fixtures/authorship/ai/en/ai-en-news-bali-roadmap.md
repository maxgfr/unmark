Delegates at the United Nations climate change conference in Bali erupted in applause on Saturday after nearly 190 countries agreed on a "Bali Roadmap" to launch negotiations on a new global climate treaty, ending two weeks of tense talks that ran a full day past their scheduled close.

The agreement sets a two-year timetable for negotiations to conclude in Copenhagen in 2009, when a successor to the Kyoto Protocol is expected to be adopted. The Kyoto accord's first commitment period expires in 2012.

The breakthrough came after the United States dropped its objections to a proposal from developing nations calling for greater support from wealthy countries on technology and finance. The US delegation had been booed on the conference floor moments earlier, and a delegate from Papua New Guinea told the Americans that if they were not willing to lead, they should "get out of the way." Shortly afterwards, the US announced it would join the consensus, prompting cheers across the hall.

The final text does not include binding emissions targets, a disappointment to the European Union and environmental groups, which had pushed for industrialised countries to cut emissions by 25 to 40 percent by 2020. Instead, that range appears only in a footnote referencing scientific findings.

UN climate chief Yvo de Boer, who had briefly left the podium in tears during an earlier session, called the outcome a "real breakthrough." UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon, who returned to Bali to urge a deal, welcomed the agreement as a vital first step.
