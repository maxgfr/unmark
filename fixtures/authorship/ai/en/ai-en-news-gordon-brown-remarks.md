Prime Minister Gordon Brown was forced onto the defensive on Wednesday after a private remark about a voter was inadvertently broadcast, describing the woman as "a sort of bigoted woman" moments after their conversation ended.

Mr Brown had been campaigning in Rochdale when he spoke with Gillian Duffy, a 65-year-old widow, who raised concerns about immigration, the national debt and the cost of living. The exchange appeared cordial, and Mr Brown thanked her before climbing into his car. However, he was still wearing a radio microphone attached to his jacket, which continued transmitting to a television crew.

Recordings of the comment quickly spread across news channels and social media. Mr Brown later apologised, calling Mrs Duffy on the telephone and then visiting her home to say sorry in person. He told reporters he had "misunderstood" what she said and described the remark as a mistake for which he took full responsibility.

Mrs Duffy, a lifelong Labour supporter, said she was "very upset" by the comment and felt let down. Opposition parties seized on the incident, arguing that it revealed a gap between the Prime Minister's public manner and private views. Labour figures insisted the episode should not overshadow the campaign's central issues, though some privately acknowledged that it was a damaging moment so close to polling day.
