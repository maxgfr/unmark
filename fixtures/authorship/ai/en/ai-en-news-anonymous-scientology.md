The loose-knit internet collective known as "Anonymous" has announced plans to hold coordinated protests outside Church of Scientology buildings around the world on February 10, escalating a campaign against the organization that began online last month.

In a video posted this week, a computer-generated voice speaking over footage of clouds called on supporters to gather peacefully at Scientology centers in cities including Los Angeles, London, Sydney, and Toronto. Organizers said participants should wear masks to protect their identities and should not engage in violence or property damage.

The group's dispute with the church started in January after Scientology attempted to remove a leaked promotional video featuring actor Tom Cruise from video-sharing sites. Anonymous members responded with denial-of-service attacks that knocked Scientology websites offline, prank calls, and faxes of black pages sent to church offices. The church described the actions as cyber-terrorism.

The February protests mark a shift toward public, real-world demonstrations. Message boards used by the group have filled with discussions of local logistics, permits, and signs. The date was chosen to coincide with the birthday of Lisa McPherson, a Scientologist who died in 1995 while in the care of church members in Clearwater, Florida. Her death became the subject of lawsuits and criminal charges that were later dropped.

A spokeswoman for the Church of Scientology said the organization respects the right to protest but characterized the group's members as "a handful of hackers" spreading hate. Police in several cities said they were aware of the planned gatherings.
