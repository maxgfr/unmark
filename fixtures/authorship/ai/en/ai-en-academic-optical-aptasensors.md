Aptasensors, biosensing devices based on aptamers—short, single-stranded DNA or RNA sequences with high binding specificity—have emerged as promising analytical tools for detecting residual antimicrobial agents in food matrices. The detection of antimicrobial residues, including antibiotics, antiparasitics, and anticonvulsants, is critical for food safety and regulatory compliance. Optical aptasensors, which harness optical properties such as fluorescence, surface plasmon resonance, or colorimetric changes, offer several advantages over conventional methods: enhanced selectivity, rapid response times, cost-effectiveness, and minimal sample preprocessing requirements. Recent advances have focused on integrating aptasensors with various nanotechnologies—including gold nanoparticles, quantum dots, and graphene—to amplify optical signals and improve detection limits. These hybrid platforms demonstrate superior sensitivity for low-concentration antimicrobial residues while maintaining species-specific recognition. Applications in rapid on-site testing and high-throughput screening have been demonstrated for common agricultural antimicrobials. Despite these advances, challenges remain in multiplexing detection, standardizing protocols across diverse food matrices, and achieving regulatory validation for commercial deployment. The growing interest in optical aptasensor development reflects the industry's urgent need for practical, accurate, and field-deployable solutions to monitor antimicrobial residues in food supply chains, particularly in response to increasing consumer concerns about food safety and antimicrobial resistance.
